Career History • Thru The Decades

Six Decades of Engineering Groundwork

From early computation for iconic suspension spans to directing structural design for John Portman and founding Ruby and Associates in 1984.

1956 – 1960

American Bridge Division & RPI Roots

Began as summer intern structural draftsman with American Bridge Division (ABD), U.S. Steel (1956–59). Upon graduating Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute (BSCE 1960), applied early computer programming and numerical analysis to critical erection/sequence calculations for the Tagus River Suspension Bridge in Portugal, closure calculations for the Astoria-Megler Bridge in Oregon, and the Cold Spring Canyon Bridge in California.

1962 – 1965

ABD Chicago Inspection Group & Heavy Steel

Transferred to Chicago design office and assigned to ABD's national inspection group. Conducted structural evaluations, blast furnace repairs, open hearth ovens, BOP steel facilities, overhead crane runways, ore bridges, rolling mills, and industrial conveyor systems across the United States.

1966 – 1971

The Supertall Revolution & The Chicago Picasso

Joined ABD High-Rise Group as Project Engineer. Responsible for connection development, erection sequencing, and derrick support structures for Chicago's 100-story John Hancock Center (1,128 ft), the 1,189-foot Standard Oil Tower (Aon Center), and the 110-story Sears Tower (Willis Tower, 1,451 ft). Also engineered the scale translation and fabrication sizing for Picasso's 50-foot CorTen steel sculpture in Chicago.

1972 – 1977

Chief Structural Engineer, John Portman & Associates

Directed structural design for architectural landmarks globally, including the Detroit Renaissance Center, World Trade Center in Brussels, the Westin Bonaventure Hotel in Los Angeles, the Westin Peachtree Plaza Hotel, and Peachtree Center towers in Atlanta.

1978 – 1983

Broad Corporation & Automotive Design/Build

Pioneered modular mezzanines and industrial steel systems in River Rouge, MI for major automotive paint systems, air supply houses, and pollution control facilities during rapid plant re-tooling and vehicle model changeovers.

1984 – Present

Ruby and Associates • 39+ Years of Excellence

Founded Ruby and Associates to pioneer constructability-focused structural steel engineering. Delivered engineering marvels including Ford Field, Hard Rock Stadium, Arizona Cardinals Stadium, Miami Marlins Park, GM M-5 Program, and Grosse Ile Toll Bridge. Ruby+Associates was acquired by Degenkolb Engineers, where Dave remains a senior constructability consultant.

Industry Benchmark Publication

AISC Design Guide #23:
Constructability of Structural Steel Buildings

Author: David I. Ruby, PE, SE, FASCE • Published by AISC (2008)

Nationally recognized by the American Institute of Steel Construction (AISC). The design guide is grounded in the core definition of Constructability established by the Construction Industry Institute (CII) at the University of Texas at Austin:

“The optimal use of construction knowledge and experience in planning, design, procurement, and field operations to achieve overall project objectives.”

Unifying The Profession

Leadership & Institutional Impact

Building bridges not only in steel, but across professional organizations to advance structural engineering nationwide.

Unifying CASE, SEI & NCSEA

As President of CASE (Coalition of American Structural Engineers), brought together the presidents of ASCE SEI and NCSEA to resolve competing programs and unify the profession into defined focal pillars: CASE on Business, SEI on Technical Training, and NCSEA on Licensure.

Co-Founder of SEAMi (1997)

Recognizing the critical need for a dedicated state organization, David Ruby and Gerd Hartung founded the Structural Engineers Association of Michigan (SEAMi), serving as Co-Presidents for the first two years to champion member cohesion and training.
